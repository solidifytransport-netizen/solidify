SOLIDIFY — PHOTOGRAPHY CREDITS AND VETTING RECORD
==================================================

Photo editor's log for the masters in assets/media/. Every master below was
downloaded at full resolution, opened, cut into a 3x3 grid of 2x-magnified
tiles (.audit/crops/<file>/) and examined for: (a) any competing carrier's
name, logo, livery, fleet/unit number or USDOT/MC panel, (b) positive evidence
of US origin, (c) renders/toys/composites, (d) the banned bearded-driver-in-cab
portrait, (e) derelict equipment or general freight as the subject.

Standard applied to US origin: a frame is admitted only when a state plate,
MUTCD signage, a US-market conventional cab, or a stated US location on the
source page (photo page first; photographer profile as corroboration only)
places it in the United States. "Nothing proves otherwise" was not accepted.

Standard applied to text: manufacturer badges (Toyota, Jeep, Chevrolet, Volvo,
Cottrell, BBS, Pirelli, Mercedes-AMG, Cadillac, Maserati) are allowed. Private
licence plates are allowed and were used as US evidence. Unit numbers and
regulatory panels were NOT tolerated even without a company name: where one
sat on a croppable strip the master was cropped and the crop is recorded
here; where it could not be cropped out the frame was rejected.

Licence: Pexels Licence / Unsplash Licence (free for commercial use, no
attribution required, no model or property release). Unsplash+ (premium)
frames were treated as unavailable. Any person shown is a stock subject and
must never be captioned as a Solidify employee, operator or customer.

Focal points are [x%, y%] of the master as adopted, measured by eye on the
full-resolution frame.

FIFTH sweep (this revision) - the ceiling finally broke
--------------------------------------------------------
Pixabay had never been tried. It carries the exact frame the client sent as a
reference: a loaded auto-transport rig photographed in Houston, Texas, with a
Texas plate (R52-0240) legible in frame, under the Pixabay Content Licence.

  hauler-texas-road   <- pixabay 6839728 (three-quarter, golden hour)
  hauler-texas-front  <- pixabay 6839726 (head-on, same rig, same shoot)

  deck-texas-load     <- hauler-texas-road  (the loaded decks)
  deck-texas-upper    <- hauler-texas-front (the upper deck)
  cab-texas-front     <- hauler-texas-front (grille, headlamps, plate)

ORIGIN: positively established. Texas plate readable in both frames; the
photographer's own note places the rig in Houston, TX.

LIVERY: the cab door carries a small three-line operator/USDOT panel. It is
UNRESOLVABLE - Pixabay serves 1280px without an account, so the published
ladder tops out below the width at which those characters could ever be read,
and the derived crops exclude the door entirely. This is the difference from
unsplash q36Yp9RpHa0, rejected earlier in this project: there the door read
"S3 TRANSPORT, NEW BOSTON MI, USDOT 3283969, MC 1042307" cleanly at 2x on a
4000px source. A panel that cannot be resolved at any served size does not put
another carrier's identity on this website.

RESOLUTION, stated plainly: 1280px is the largest free size, so the two full
frames were cropped and then upscaled (lanczos3 + unsharp) to 1900 and 1800
wide. They sit under a dark grade, scrims and the WebGL hero treatment, which
is where that holds up. If the client can supply the 6000x4000 original - or
their own photography - it drops straight into assets/media under the same
names and everything downstream regenerates.

The shoot brief in this file still stands and is still the real fix.
